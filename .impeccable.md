## Design Context

### Users
Turn-based tactics players and the two RIGBOUND founders, using the game both as a playable strategy experience and a rapid combat-design workbench. The interface must make positioning, terrain, targets, damage, and cause-and-effect legible at a glance.

### Brand Personality
Tactile, severe, and kinetic. RIGBOUND should feel like a compact mechanical diorama under battlefield pressure: readable enough for deliberate planning, but explosive and satisfying when an action resolves.

### Aesthetic Direction
An original isometric tactics language strongly informed by the supplied Into the Breach desert reference: chunky tile silhouettes, dark seams, cracked sun-baked surfaces, sparse structures, and hard tactical contrast. Environmental effects take inspiration from the supplied pixel-art lava and water references: luminous layered fluids, bright contact foam, particulate spray, bubbles, heat, and restrained bloom against deep shadow. Avoid generic neon sci-fi panels, excessive glass, smooth toy-like terrain, and visual noise that obscures the grid.

### Design Principles
1. Tactical information wins: tile boundaries, units, threats, and impact direction remain readable through every effect.
2. Build effects in three scales: a sharp primary flash, material particles, and a slower environmental after-effect.
3. Give terrain material identity through silhouette and motion, not texture noise alone.
4. Use a limited earthy battlefield palette; reserve high-chroma light for water, energy, attacks, and state changes.
5. Make iteration visible and immediate through in-game preview tools and data-driven recipes.
